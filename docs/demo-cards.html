The problem a bridge cannot avoid

Mina cannot read Flare.

A zkApp has no way to look at another chain. Someone must tell it what happened — and the whole security of the bridge is who, and whether you had to take their word for it.

Our answer is nobody we chose: Flare's own validator set, the one already securing the chain.

Flare Data Connector

The chain attests to
its own history.

Flare's validators vote on an attestation request each round and sign the Merkle root of the result. We take that root, the Merkle proof, and the signatures — and verify all of it inside the Mina zkApp, against the signing policy Mina holds on-chain.

Mina never trusts our relayer. It checks Flare's validator signatures itself.

Where this goes next

Today the proving is one-way.
It does not have to stay that way.

Mina is a proof system with a chain attached — recursion, constant-size state, cheap verification of arbitrary computation.

The same rail that carries assets can carry proofs: a Mina zkApp proving something expensive off-chain, verified on Flare for a fraction of running it there. Flare gets an asset today, and a proving layer to draw on tomorrow.
